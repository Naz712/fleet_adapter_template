The same casualty, folded into a chair shape on the 3-part platform, with the platform hinged on top of the battery box and kept level by the linear actuator. It is carried up and down the stairs four ways: on tracks that can't bend, on tracks in 2 parts, on tracks in 4 parts, and on a stiff track with arms at the front that swing up and down, like the Cal Poly Pomona stair climber. The bending tracks stop at each edge when a laser at the front sees the drop, bend until they touch the stairs, then drive on. How it is built: 2 parts, and the 4-part version

Two track units joined at the middle axle, which is both the hinge and the driven axle. The drawings are full size, in mm; the 1:5 prototype is the same with every size divided by 5.

How the hinge works

  1. One axle, two units. The rear and front units both turn on the middle axle, on bearings in their side plates: the rear unit’s plates inside, the front unit’s just outside them.
  2. The hinge motor swings the front unit. A self-locking worm gear motor in the box turns a cross shaft with a small gear at each end. Each gear meshes with a toothed sector on one of the front unit’s side plates, so the front unit swings up or down about the middle axle, up to 55°, at 30° a second.
  3. It holds with the power off. A worm can’t be turned backwards by the load, so the bend stays where the motor left it.
  4. The tracks drive at any bend. The drive motor turns the middle axle through a chain. Both units’ belts run on pulleys clamped to that axle, and bending about it doesn’t stretch or slacken them, so both tracks keep driving whatever the bend. The end axles turn freely.
  5. The platform sits on top. It pivots on pillow blocks on the box, right above the middle axle. The platform and its levelling are designed separately.

When it bends: stop, bend, then go

  1. Drive with the hinge locked. The laser at the leading end looks straight down at the stairs.
  2. See the edge, stop. When the laser reads 1¼ risers (219 mm) further down than on flat ground, the vehicle stops.
  3. Bend until it touches. The hinge motor folds the leading unit down over an edge, or up at the foot of a flight, until it touches the stairs. It only pushes the track onto the stairs, lifting the vehicle 5 cm at most.
  4. Drive on. The other unit follows over the edge and the hinge straightens as it goes, stopping again only if it would have to turn faster than 30° a second.
